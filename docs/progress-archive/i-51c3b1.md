## What was built

- **The C=O checks** (`carbonylChecks()`, right after HYDROXY_PREFIX, before the parent choice; each rule 1, step `group`,
  «Reconoce el grupo funcional», whose text says which group is principal, how it is written and, beside other oxygen
  groups, the seniority):
  - **CARBONYL_SENIORITY** — the student's suffix is a junior group's (`seniorityRank()`): `ona` beside an aldehyde, `ol`
    or `fenol` beside a C=O. The order, the principal group and its ending (or `benzaldehído`), and the student's group as
    the prefix the engine gives it (`oxo`, `hidroxi`, lexicon GROUP_PREFIXES) when its carbon is on the engine's parent,
    «en los prefijos» otherwise (`3-formilhexan-2-ona` → `2-acetilpentanal`). Focus: the student's suffix groups; tiles:
    the suffix.
  - **KETONE_NOT_ALDEHYDE** — the student's `al` (or an aldehyde template's –CHO) on a carbon bonded to two carbons by a
    branch of the student's (`1-metilpropanal`, `1-acetilhexanal`, `1-metilbutanodial` → `4-oxopentanal`,
    `1-(4-metilfenil)acetaldehído`, `dimetilformaldehído`). Names the branch and the carbon. Focus: the C=O and the branch.
  - **ALDEHYDE_CARBON** — the –CHO's carbon on the other side of the parent: `carbaldehído` after a chain
    (`butano-1-carbaldehído` → `pentanal`, `butano-1,4-dicarbaldehído` → `hexanodial`), or the –CHO's carbon as the
    student's chain beside a ring (`ciclohexilformaldehído`, `ciclohexilmetanal`, `fenilformaldehído` → `benzaldehído`).
  - **CARBONYL_PREFIX** — HYDROXY_PREFIX's analogue: a parent-level prefix whose own C=O (an `oxo`; the C=O of a `formil`
    or acyl prefix, `ownCarbonylOxygen()`) the engine cites in the suffix, while the student's suffix cites fewer groups
    (`2-oxopropano`, `oxociclohexano`, `formilbenceno`, `5-oxopentanal`, `oxoacetaldehído`, `4-oxociclohexan-1-ona`,
    `1,3-diformilciclohexano`). For `formil` / acyl prefixes whose carbon joins the engine's chain: «Su carbono entra en la
    cadena principal», plus «y el anillo pasa a ser una rama» when the student's parent was a ring (`acetilbenceno`,
    `butanoilciclohexano`, `4-acetiltolueno`, `2-acetilpentano`, `1-acetilpentano-2,4-diona` → `heptano-2,4,6-triona`).
  - After the four: a student's suffix of another kind than the engine's, or with its –CHO on the other side, that none
    names is a null (`BEYOND`; no case found in any sweep). A suffix with fewer groups, or none, goes on to the rules.
- **CARBONYL_PARENT** (rule 1, `principalParent()`, HYDROXY_PARENT generalised): the student's chain against the engine's
  ring, or the reverse; the counts of the `RINGCHAIN` step; step `ringChain` (`benzene` / `ring` without it).
- **MORE_CARBONYL_GROUPS** (rule 2, P0, step `groupChain`): `chainDropped()` and `fewerPrincipalGroups()` (also before
  BRANCH_AT_END, as for an alcohol).
- **N0** (`numberingReason()`, `lowerLocants()`, the data's `group`): NUMBERING, RING_NUMBERING, CHAIN_LOCANTS and
  ETHER_SIDE's CHAIN_LOCANTS reason with «grupos –CHO» / «grupos C=O»; the OH phrases unchanged.
- **NEEDLESS_LOCANTS** (`suffixWhat()`): «el grupo C=O», «el grupo –CHO» and plurals.
- **ETHER_SIDE** (`ACROSS_OXYGEN`): CARBONYL_PARENT and MORE_CARBONYL_GROUPS told from the oxygen
  (`(2-oxopropoxi)metano`, `(2-oxociclohexiloxi)metano`), `etherSide()` naming each principal group.

## Decisions

- **Codes: new where the existing name would mislabel, variants where the code is generic.** HYDROXY_PARENT and
  MORE_HYDROXY_GROUPS are named after the OH, so their C=O forms are new codes (CARBONYL_PARENT, MORE_CARBONYL_GROUPS)
  sharing their logic, rule and step; NUMBERING / RING_NUMBERING / CHAIN_LOCANTS at N0 and NEEDLESS_LOCANTS are generic,
  so they get group-keyed phrases. The four checks say things no message says: four codes.
- **ESO wording «grupo –CHO» / «grupo C=O»**, as the explanation's own steps (SUFFIX_GROUP_WORDS), rather than «grupo
  carbonilo»; the seniority «aldehído > cetona > alcohol» as the group step words it.
- **Order** `HYDROXY_PREFIX → CARBONYL_SENIORITY → KETONE_NOT_ALDEHYDE → ALDEHYDE_CARBON → CARBONYL_PREFIX →
  principalParent → template / chain / ring rules`: the explanation names the principal group first; seniority before the
  prefix check so `5-oxopentan-2-ona` explains why the aldehyde wins; KETONE_NOT_ALDEHYDE before CARBONYL_PREFIX so
  `1-acetilhexanal` blames the branch on the –CHO's carbon; ALDEHYDE_CARBON before the parent choice so
  `ciclohexilformaldehído` does not say «las cadenas no llevan ninguno» of the –CHO's own carbon. The DIAGNOSES order
  follows it (each beside the alcohol code whose place it takes).
- **CARBONYL_PREFIX only while the student's suffix cites fewer groups than the engine's.** Without that limit
  `4-propanoilheptano-2,6-diona` (two ketones in the student's suffix, the chain choice wrong at N0) was told its groups
  go in the suffix; it is now CHAIN_LOCANTS at N0. An `oxo` on the student's parent always meets the limit.
- **A C=O inside a compound branch is not CARBONYL_PREFIX** (as a `hidroxi` there is not HYDROXY_PREFIX): the parent
  choice speaks (`(4-formilfenil)metano`, `(2-oxoetil)ciclohexano` → CARBONYL_PARENT; `(4-acetilfenil)metano` →
  MORE_CARBONYL_GROUPS).
- **`naming/seniority.js` import:** `seniorityRank()` is the engine's own order (never retyped). The reverse-purity test's
  allowlist gains it for `diagnose.js` only (design §15.4 forbids the engine's grouping and rendering to the assembler).

## Review fix

Codex review (`docs/reviews/I-51c3b1.md`): one should-fix, no blockers.

- **Should-fix (`diagnose.js` `aldehydeCarbon()`): ALDEHYDE_CARBON misfired on a ring-or-chain parent choice.**
  `2-(4-formilciclohexil)etanal` (→ `4-(2-oxoetil)ciclohexano-1-carbaldehído`) said the highlighted –CHO is bonded to
  the ring, but the student's `al` ends the side chain; the engine's suffix is the ring's other –CHO, and the mistake is
  the parent choice (one –CHO each, a tie: the ring). Cause: the check compared only whether the two suffixes are
  `carbaldehído`, never which groups they are. Fix: ALDEHYDE_CARBON requires every –CHO carbon of the student's suffix to
  be one of the engine's suffix –CHO carbons (`aldehydeCarbons()`, the `carbaldehído` group's own carbon or the carrying
  carbon). Otherwise the check passes, and so does the leftover null of `carbonylChecks()`, which now covers only a
  suffix of another kind than the engine's. The parent rule then speaks: CARBONYL_PARENT for both directions
  (`2-(4-formilciclohexil)etanal`, `3-(4-formilciclohexil)propanal`: the ring on a tie;
  `4-(1,3-dioxopropan-2-il)ciclohexano-1-carbaldehído` → `2-(4-formilciclohexil)propanodial`: the chain with more –CHO
  groups). The genuine cases keep ALDEHYDE_CARBON (`butano-1-carbaldehído`, `ciclohexilformaldehído`,
  `(4-formilciclohexil)metanal` → `ciclohexano-1,4-dicarbaldehído`, `4-(3-oxopropil)butano-1-carbaldehído` →
  `octanodial`). Header «Aldehydes and ketones» updated.
- **Same-kind scan of the other codes:** KETONE_NOT_ALDEHYDE points at the student's own `al` carbon, bonded to two
  carbons by construction, so it is always a ketone. CARBONYL_SENIORITY points at the student's own suffix groups, junior
  by kind, with `oxo` / `hidroxi` named only when their carbon is on the engine's parent. CARBONYL_PREFIX already matches
  by identity (the prefix's own C=O oxygen is one of the engine's suffix oxygens). Ring and side-chain probes of each
  (`1-(4-formilciclohexil)etan-1-ona`, `4-(2-oxopropil)ciclohexan-1-ol`, `2-(4-formilciclohexil)-1-metiletanal`,
  `1-(4-oxociclohexil)propan-2-ona`) gave truthful messages: no guard needed.
- **Tests:** a new test in `reverse-diagnose-carbonyls.test.js` (9 tests): the three parent choices (code, trace rule,
  step, message, and the student's –CHO not among the engine's) and the four genuine ALDEHYDE_CARBON cases.
