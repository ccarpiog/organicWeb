## What was built

- **The nitrile checks** (`nitrileChecks()`, first in `firstRule()` as before, now exclusive with esterChecks() / acidChecks() by
  the engine's principal group; each rule 1, step `group`, «Reconoce el grupo funcional», which says the –C≡N is one group whose
  triple bond is no alkyne's, how it is written and, beside other groups, the whole seniority):
  - **CARBONYL_SENIORITY** (extended, `group` 'nitrile') — a junior suffix beside the –C≡N (`4-cianobutan-2-ona` →
    `4-oxopentanonitrilo`, `3-cianopropanal`, `4-cianobutan-2-ol`, `4-cianofenol`, `4-cianobenzaldehído` → «formil»), with the
    order «ácido > éster > amida > nitrilo > aldehído > cetona > alcohol > amina» (`nitrileSeniority`, the nitrile group step's).
  - **CARBONYL_SENIORITY with the junior `nitrile`** (acidChecks(), esterChecks()) — a student's nitrile suffix beside an acid's
    –COOH or an ester's –COO– (`3-carboxipropanonitrilo` → `ácido 3-cianopropanoico`, `4-carboxibenzonitrilo`,
    `3-(metoxicarbonil)propanonitrilo` → `3-cianopropanoato de metilo`, `2-(acetiloxi)etanonitrilo` → `etanoato de
    cianometilo`): the –C≡N becomes `ciano` when its carbon hangs from the parent or the O-bound group's chain (juniorPrefix()),
    and the message adds that `ciano` includes its carbon, which then counts in no chain (`cyanoCarbon`: «…, que incluye su
    carbono: ese carbono no se cuenta en la cadena principal» / «en el anillo» / «en la cadena de ese grupo»); the order is the
    one the acid's or the ester's step gives beside a `ciano` (`cyanoSeniority`).
  - **KETONE_NOT_ALDEHYDE** (reused) — an `al` on a carbon a `ciano` or a branch bonds to a second carbon (`1-cianobutanal` →
    `2-oxopentanonitrilo`; the `ciano`, a hetero group, is not named as «la rama»).
  - **NITRILE_CARBON** (new, ESTER_CARBON's twin through `groupCarbon()`) — `carbonitrilo` on a chain, or the –C≡N's carbon as the
    student's chain beside a ring, only for the engine's own –C≡N groups.
  - **CARBONYL_PREFIX** (extended, `ownGroupAtom()`) — a parent-level `ciano` whose nitrogen is one of the engine's suffix
    nitrogens while the student's suffix cites fewer –C≡N groups (templates included); «Su carbono entra en la cadena principal»
    on a chain (`joins`), never beside a ring.
- **The rules with the –C≡N's words** (`group` 'nitrile' through NAMED_GROUPS): CARBONYL_PARENT, MORE_CARBONYL_GROUPS (P0, also
  before BRANCH_AT_END), ETHER_SIDE told from either, RING_NUMBERING / NUMBERING / CHAIN_LOCANTS at N0, NEEDLESS_LOCANTS
  (`suffixWhat()`), MISSING_LOCANTS for a suffix locant.
- **How the –C≡N is written** (`DIAGNOSIS_PHRASES.ending()`, `principalEnding()`): «con la terminación «-nitrilo», al final del
  nombre» (`-dinitrilo`, `-carbonitrilo`, `-tricarbonitrilo`…), or «con «benzonitrilo» (el benceno con un grupo –C≡N)».
- **`groupCarbonText()`**: ALDEHYDE_CARBON, ACID_CARBON, ESTER_CARBON and NITRILE_CARBON share one text (the earlier three
  byte-identical, their tests unchanged).

## Decisions

- **Codes: the shared ones where the message holds, one new code where it must differ** (the acid's and the ester's precedent).
  The nitrile has no C=O, but the student reads only the message, which names the –C≡N, and the brief asks the shared codes'
  reuse; so the CARBONYL_* codes (internal names) take `group` 'nitrile'. CARBONYL_GROUPS keeps its meaning (the groups with a
  C=O) and the new NAMED_GROUPS (C=O groups + nitrile) is what the shared phrases key on. One new code, **NITRILE_CARBON**:
  ESTER_CARBON is named after the ester, as ACID_CARBON after the acid; its text says «-carbonitrilo» and «–C≡N». No
  **NITRILE_GROUP** (the counterpart of ACID_GROUP / ESTER_GROUP): no tile writes a –C≡N's nitrogen apart, so it cannot be split.
- **Order** `CARBONYL_SENIORITY → KETONE_NOT_ALDEHYDE → NITRILE_CARBON → CARBONYL_PREFIX → principalParent → rules`, the acid's
  without its split check; DIAGNOSES puts NITRILE_CARBON after ESTER_CARBON. nitrileChecks() stays first in firstRule(): it acts
  only when the engine's principal group is a –C≡N, so it never meets the ester's or the acid's checks.
- **The junior nitrile's wording (the I-52b1 concern).** A `ciano` is a hetero group whose carbon the engine never counts in a
  chain (IUPAC 2013 P-66.5); the acid's CARBONYL_SENIORITY words for an `oxo` keep the junior carbon in the chain. So the
  nitrile junior gets its own phrase, as the explanation's cyanoCarbonSentence() says it: «…con el prefijo «ciano», que incluye
  su carbono: ese carbono no se cuenta en la cadena principal» (the student's `nitrilo` had made it carbon 1), «en el anillo» on
  a ring parent, «en la cadena de ese grupo» on an ester's O-bound group. The acid's carbon entering the chain is shown by the
  «Se escribe» name and the step, as for every acid seniority message.
- **Seniority wording, from the steps the messages open:** the nitrile group step always says the whole order, «ácido > éster >
  amida > nitrilo > aldehído > cetona > alcohol > amina» (asserted equal to naming/seniority.js SENIORITY in Spanish words); the
  acid's and the ester's steps list «nitrilo» beside a `ciano` («ácido > nitrilo > aldehído > cetona > alcohol», «ácido > éster >
  nitrilo > …»). The acid's and the ester's existing phrases are unchanged.
- **«grupo –C≡N» with the dash** in every diagnosis phrase (the explanation's SUFFIX_GROUP_WORDS); the parser's and builder's
  «grupo C≡N» untouched.
- **NUMBERING and CHAIN_LOCANTS at N0 are worded but unreachable for a nitrile** (a –C≡N's carbon ends any chain that holds it,
  and `nitrilo` puts it at 1, as an acid's): no sweep and no random name (≈30 000) reached them.
- **MISSING_LOCANTS for a suffix locant** names «cada grupo –C≡N»; alone it is the verdict `missingLocants`, so only diagnose()
  on such a verdict says it (as for the acid and the ester).
- **The one null left** (BEYOND): a student's nitrile suffix while the engine's principal group is junior to a –C≡N. It cannot be
  built (a student's `nitrilo` builds a –C≡N, outranked only by an acid's or an ester's group among the groups the tiles write;
  those checks name the junior nitrile), so it is tested with a made-up naming, as the acid's one null.
- **Test category refined:** `nitrileCategory()` read `carbon` from the suffix's outside-ness alone; it now also requires the
  student's –C≡N carbons to be the engine's (groupCarbon()'s condition), so `2-(4-cianociclohexil)etanonitrilo` (another –C≡N
  than the ring's) is `parent`, CARBONYL_PARENT. No sweep count changed.
- **Chemistry:** nothing from memory; the seniority is naming/seniority.js's, every name and form the forward engine's.
