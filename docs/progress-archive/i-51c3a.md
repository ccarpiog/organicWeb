## What was built

- **Final template list (tile order):** `formaldehído` (metanal), `acetaldehído` (etanal), `acetona` (propanona), `acetofenona`
  (1-feniletan-1-ona), `fenilacetaldehído` (2-feniletanal), `ftalaldehído`, `isoftalaldehído`, `tereftalaldehído`
  (benceno-1,2/1,3/1,4-dicarbaldehído) — every retained carbonyl word the engine emits (principal.js carbonylTraditionalId(),
  aromatic.js retainedDigroupIds(); `acetaldehído` also the `arylacetaldehyde` word). Ids `template-formaldehyde` …
  `template-terephthalaldehyde`; texts from `TRADITIONAL_NAMES` and `BENZENE_DIGROUP_NAMES`.
- **Model:** a template's principal group is now general (templateSuffix(): the OH groups of R-5a3a, or a carbonyl template's
  `suffix`, a SUFFIX_GROUPS key with its carbons). The five chain templates have `chain` carbons: interpret.js reads them as a
  saturated open chain (ParentSpec kind 'chain', the TemplateSpec kept), numbered as the systematic name (the C=O / –CHO carbon at
  1, `acetona`'s at 2); the C=O is the SuffixSpec placed by the word (implicit slots, `placed`), so build.js, search.js, the
  verdict's `suffixAtoms` and the diagnosis treat it as the engine's principal group. `acetofenona` and `fenilacetaldehído` have
  a phenyl template group (at 1 / at 2). The ftalaldehídos are benzene templates with two outside –CHO, like `benzaldehído`'s.
- **Parser:** numbers or a letter on any carbonyl template are TEMPLATE_LOCANTS (««acetona» ya dice dónde va su grupo C=O, en el
  carbono 2…»); a lettered prefix on a ftalaldehído counts its two –CHO (POSITIONAL «contando los dos grupos CHO del
  ftalaldehído»); on a chain template it is POSITIONAL `notBenzene`; inside a compound branch, a hint to write `oxo` / `formil`.
- **Builder:** LOCANT_RANGE on a chain template speaks of its chain («La cadena de la acetona tiene 3 carbonos…», «El
  formaldehído solo tiene 1 carbono…»), and for `acetofenona` / `fenilacetaldehído` adds that the ring is not numbered there and
  gives the systematic name with a ring group (which the app reads); VALENCE says «grupo C=O» for a ketone template.
- **Diagnosis:** SUBSTITUTED_TEMPLATE on a chain template (substitutedChainTemplate(), before the chain's and the ring's rules,
  step `groupChain`), worded without a parent; on a ring template ringRules() skips it where the engine admits the prefixes.
- **Ayuda:** the intro sentence now ends «…, y también de sus éteres, aldehídos y cetonas.»; a new «Aldehídos y cetonas»
  paragraph (al, ona, carbaldehído, benzaldehído, oxo, formil, acetil, oil, carbonil, benzoil, the retained names and which take
  prefixes).

## Decisions

- **Substituted-template policy (from the code):** the engine writes `formaldehído`, `acetona`, `acetofenona` and
  `fenilacetaldehído` for the bare molecules only, the ftalaldehídos with any prefixes (`substitutable`: `4-metilftalaldehído`,
  numbered like the systematic name), and `acetaldehído` with one phenyl on carbon 2 (bare: the word `fenilacetaldehído`; with
  its own prefixes: `2-(4-metilfenil)acetaldehído`). So engine-written forms are `alternative` by the exact comparison (nothing
  special in readName()), and tiles.js templateAdmits() tells the diagnosis which prefixes are no SUBSTITUTED_TEMPLATE mistake:
  any on a ftalaldehído, one non-alkoxy, non-acyl phenyl on `acetaldehído`. Every other prefixed carbonyl template is
  `otherName` with SUBSTITUTED_TEMPLATE (or `ambiguous` / `missingLocants` with left-out locants, as before).
- **Chain templates are open chains, prefixes numbered on the chain** (`2-(4-metilfenil)acetaldehído`, as the engine numbers
  it). So `4-metilacetofenona` (a school name numbering the ring) is LOCANT_RANGE, whose message points at
  `1-(4-metilfenil)etan-1-ona`, which reads as `correct` (no dead end).
- **`fenilacetaldehído` is its own tile** (the brief's list; the engine's one word). Typed as `fenil` + `acetaldehído`, the same
  text reads as the same `alternative`; the tokenizer returns the single tile (longest surface form first).
- **SUBSTITUTED_TEMPLATE runs after outsideRules()**, as for the retained phenols (`4-hidroxicatecol` stayed null in I-51a3a): a
  substituted carbonyl template whose suffix is not the engine's stays a carbonyl null for I-51c3b.
- **The carbonyl fixture sweep now mutates the template names too** (as the alcohol sweep does the phenol templates): swept names
  92 (was 84); one more `rotated` otherName and one more `suffix` null (the aryl of `2-(4-metilfenil)acetaldehído` rotated onto
  carbon 1: a ketone).
- **Ftalaldehído keys and ids:** keyed by their BENZENE_DIGROUP_NAMES id, no `id` (as the diols); chain templates carry their
  TRADITIONAL_NAMES id.

## Deviations

- The brief says «`alternative`, or `correct` where it is the name shown first»: the engine shows none of these names first, so
  all are `alternative`; the systematic names are `correct` and announce them («¡Correcto! También se llama «acetona» …»), as
  before this phase.
- `cloroacetaldehído` (left-out locant) is `ambiguous` «Faltan números: puede ser 2-cloroetanal u otra molécula.»: the engine
  writes no `2-cloroacetaldehído`, so no filled text matches (step 8 lists the engine's names; the chlorine on carbon 1, an acyl
  chloride, is refused and counted as «otra molécula»). Pre-existing step-8 behaviour; `cloroacetona` is one structure,
  `otherName` with SUBSTITUTED_TEMPLATE.
- SUBSTITUTED_TEMPLATE gained a chain wording (`parent` null in its data) and a draft `step` (`groupChain`); its DIAGNOSES entry
  and benzene wording are unchanged.
