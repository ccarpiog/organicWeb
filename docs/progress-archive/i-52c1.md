## What was built

- **Tiles:** «Grupos» gains `amina` at its end (a suffix like `ol`: one nitrogen per group, single-bonded to its carbon);
  «Números» gains `N` after 1–12 (always shown). The `N` tile is a locant of its own kind; its value is the engine's N_LOCANT, 0,
  below every carbon locant, so a mixed list sorts as the engine cites it (`N,2-dimetil`).
- **Grammar:** `locant = NUM | 1(n) | N`. The parser reads `N` wherever a number is (so the tree stays lossless) and
  checkNitrogen() keeps it to the locants of a name-level prefix of a name whose parent carries `amina`; anywhere else
  TILE_ORDER with one of two hints. No new error code.
- **Assembler / tokenizer:** nothing of their own: `amina` starts with a vowel, so rule 4 elides the parent's last vowel and a
  multiplier's `a` before it (`etanamina`, `bencen-1-amina`, `butano-1,2,3,4-tetramina`, but `butano-1,4-diamina`), as
  render.js suffixWords() does; `N` is punctuated as a locant; the tokenizer reads `amina` as OL and `N` as NUM.
- **Interpreter / builder:** an `N` slot is a carbon slot of the parent with the value 0 and the domain `[0, n]`; build.js bonds
  its branch to the amine's nitrogen (the first group's: with two amines any `N` is the engine's `substitutedPolyamine`
  refusal) and checks the nitrogen's three bonds (VALENCE «El nitrógeno de la amina tendría 4 enlaces, y un nitrógeno solo
  puede tener 3.», blamed on the `N` that adds the fourth). A halogen, an alkoxy, an `iliden` or a ring on the nitrogen of a ring
  amine are built and refused by the engine (`outOfScope`); an acyl group on it is an amide the engine names (`otherName`).
- **Search:** a left-out slot may take `N` when the amine is one group and the prefix is a carbon branch bonded by a single bond
  through a carbon of its own (a chain or ring ending in `il`, `fenil`, `isopropil`, `tert-butil`); the nitrogen's two free
  bonds prune at index 0. The filled name writes the `N` tile first and, beside an `N`, every name-level prefix's locant;
  fillVariants() tries the suffix's list alone without locants, as the engine omits it beside an `N` (`N-metiletanamina`).
- **Diagnosis (`diagnose.js`, header «Amines»):** outsideRules() lets amines through; amineChecks() runs first and answers an
  AMINE_NULLS draft for the amine's own mistakes (`seniority`, `count`, `side`); the guards give `parent` (principalParent(),
  P0) and `numbering` (N0) and `suffix` (the suffix's needless or missing locants) nulls. Every other rule runs and was checked
  to say something true of an amine name: BRANCH_AT_END (a group on the nitrogen never lengthens the chain), LONGER_CHAIN,
  MORE_SUBSTITUENTS, CHAIN_LOCANTS, NUMBERING, RING_NUMBERING (N1–N4, the `N` groups at `N` in every numbering),
  BRANCH_RING_NUMBERING, ALPHABETICAL_ORDER, UNGROUPED (`«N,N-dimetil», no «N-metil-N-metil»`), MULTIPLIER_KIND, LOCANT_ORDER,
  NEEDLESS_LOCANTS inside a branch and for the carbon prefixes, OLD_STYLE («…justo delante de «amina»…»).
- **Dialog:** the two tiles with tooltips; `N` in the locant colour.

## Decisions

- **`N` is the value 0 of a carbon slot, not a role of its own** (the forward engine's own N_LOCANT): ranges, multisets, the
  student's order, the ascending check of LOCANT_ORDER and the filled name all treat it as the locant below 1, exactly as
  numbering.js ranks it; only its domain is widened (`[0, n]`) and build.js maps 0 to the nitrogen.
- **`N` misplaced is a parse error, its chemistry the builder's and the engine's:** checkNitrogen() refuses `N` where no amine
  nitrogen is named (TILE_ORDER, no new code); a group the engine never puts on an amine's N is built and judged by the engine
  (a halogen or an oxygen there: its HETEROATOM refusal; an acyl group: the amide it names), so the student always gets the
  molecule the tiles describe.
- **Left-out `N` only for the groups the engine puts on an amine's N, and only on a one-group amine** (§15.5.3 step 2: `N` only
  for N-substituents): `cloroetanamina` is searched on carbons only; a diamine's substituted nitrogen is always refused.
- **The suffix's locants alone left out are a fill variant for amines only** (render.js omitsPrefixLocants() counts the
  omission without the groups on N while the `N` locant and the carbon prefixes' are always cited): without it
  `metiletanamina` would have been `ambiguous` instead of `missingLocants` «N-metiletanamina».
- **The amine's nulls are ranked drafts, not BEYOND.** Most correct amine names leave the suffix's locant out (`etanamina`,
  `N-metil-N-metiletanamina`), so every placement of a symmetric parent is a reading; a BEYOND on the reading that puts the amine
  on the other end (an N0 numbering null) would have nulled the kinder reading's UNGROUPED. AMINE_NULLS keep the rule number of
  the mistake (seniority 1, side / parent 2, numbering 4, suffix 9), diagnose() keeps the latest rule among the readings as
  before, and answers null when that is an amine null. The other families' BEYOND are unchanged.
- **amineChecks() comes first, before the nitrile's, the ester's and the acid's checks:** an amine is junior to every other
  group the app names, so a student's `amina` beside one of their groups must not reach their seniority code, whose words are
  the other family's (`3-cianopropan-1-amina`, `3-oxobutan-1-amina` → seniority nulls).
- **`bencen-1-amina` alone is `otherName`** (the engine writes `bencenamina`, with no `bencen-1-amina` alternative), a `suffix`
  null for I-52c4; `4-metilbencen-1-amina` is correct.
- **Chemistry:** nothing from memory; every name, form and refusal is the forward engine's.
