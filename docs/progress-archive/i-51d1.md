## What was built

- **Tiles:** «Grupos» gains, at its end, `ácido` (kind `suffixClass`), `carboxi` (a retained word), `oico` and
  `carboxílico` (suffixes); «Anillos» gains `benzoico` after `benzaldehído`. Every text from the lexicon. SUFFIX_GROUPS
  `acid` (terminal, like `al`) and `carboxylic` (outside, like `carbaldehído`), both `hydroxy`; GROUP_WORD_PARENTS `benzoic`
  (its –COOH at carbon 1, «grupo COOH»).
- **Grammar:** `name = [ácido], {prefix}, parent`; `suffix = … | oico | carboxílico`; `parent = … | benzoico`; `simple = … |
  carboxi`. The tree keeps `ácido` as `suffixClass` (first tile only). ACID_WORD: `ácido` with no acid suffix (on `ácido`),
  or an acid suffix with no `ácido` anywhere (on the suffix's or `benzoico`'s tile); an `ácido` out of place is TILE_ORDER
  alone. `oico` mirrors `al`: no locants (TILE_ORDER, also old-style ones that could only be its: `ácido 1-butanoico`), old
  style goes to the segment (`ácido 2-butenoico`), TILE_ORDER after a ring or `benceno`. `carboxílico` takes locants as
  `carbaldehído`, and after a chain it is read (the engine judges it). `benzoico` is `benzaldehído`'s twin (TEMPLATE_LOCANTS,
  counted by an o/m/p letter: `ácido p-metilbenzoico`), its messages quoting «ácido benzoico».
- **Assembler:** rule 9 — `ácido` (part kind 'ending', as render.js), then the automatic space (a 'punct' span, only when
  something follows); rule 4 already elides `ano` before `oico` and keeps it before `dioico` and `carboxílico`. 32 forms are
  checked against the engine's own text and tokenized back.
- **Tokenizer:** `ácido` only at the start (class ACID), then exactly its space (ACIDSPACE), after which the name reads as
  from the start (letters included).
- **Interpreter / builder:** NameSpec `classWord` `{kind: 'acid', text, tiles}`; SuffixSpec `hydroxy`. Build puts the =O
  and an OH on the –COOH's carbon: the chain end for `oico` (three bonds of it; implicit slots at 1 and, with `dioico`, the
  last carbon), a new carbon outside for `carboxílico` and `benzoico`; `suffixAtoms` entries get `hydroxy`/`hydroxyBond`
  (carried onto the drawn graph by `placementReading()`). `carboxi` is a retained template: one carbon, its =O and its OH.
  The caps count the –COOH's carbon and both oxygens. CARBONYL_POSITION (reused) for more –COOH groups of `oico` than the
  chain has ends; VALENCE on a `oico` end names «oico»; on `benzoico`'s carbon 1, «…en el ácido benzoico, ese carbono ya
  lleva un grupo COOH».
- **Search:** no new role; `suffixBondAmount()` (shared with build.js): three bonds for `oico`, one for `carboxílico`.
- **Diagnosis (`diagnose.js`, header «Acids»):** `outsideRules()` lets the acids through; `acidChecks()` runs first in
  `firstRule()` and returns BEYOND (the reading's null) for the acid's own mistakes; BEYOND guards in `chainDropped()` (P0,
  N0), `fewerPrincipalGroups()`, `directionDraft()` (N0), `principalParent()` and `needlessLocants()` (the suffix's
  locants), propagated by `firstRule()`. Every other rule runs and was checked to say something true of an acid name:
  BRANCH_AT_END, LONGER_CHAIN, MORE_SUBSTITUENTS, CHAIN_LOCANTS (N1–N4), NUMBERING, RING_NUMBERING (N1–N4), BENZENE_RING,
  BRANCH_RING_NUMBERING, ACYL_NAME, ETHER_SIDE, ALPHABETICAL_ORDER (`carboxi…` under c), UNGROUPED, MULTIPLIER_KIND,
  LOCANT_ORDER, OLD_STYLE («eno» o «ino»; «dicarboxílico»). The class-name track now keys on the `éter` parent
  (`isClassName()`), so `ácido` never reaches it. `carboxi`'s shape for the prefix style is the engine's carboxy prefix's
  (`X:COOH`).
- **Dialog:** the five tiles with tooltips; `ácido` in the endings' colour (`.nb-tile--suffixClass`).

## Decisions

- **`ácido` is a kind of its own (`suffixClass`), not `classWord`.** `classWord` is the `éter` parent node of an ether's
  class name; `ácido` comes first and the substitutive name follows it, so the tree keeps it apart (`suffixClass`) and the
  NameSpec's `classWord` carries `{kind: 'acid'}` as interpret.js had foreseen. The diagnosis's class-name track, which read
  `spec.classWord`, now reads the tree's `éter` parent.
- **One new parse code, ACID_WORD, both ways**, each with its message and tile (ESO wording); an `ácido` out of place keeps
  only its TILE_ORDER, so the student is never told two things about one tile.
- **`oico` is `al`'s twin, `carboxílico` `carbaldehído`'s, `benzoico` `benzaldehído`'s, `carboxi` `formil`'s**: every R-5c1
  path is reused (`isTerminalSuffix()`, `terminalHints()`), only the wording differs.
- **CARBONYL_POSITION is reused** for the acids' terminal positions (`ácido metanodioico`, `ácido butanotrioico`) with
  –COOH wording, rather than a new build code.
- **SUFFIX_GROUPS keeps its earlier entries unchanged**; the acid entries add `hydroxy: true`, and `suffixBondAmount()`
  turns an entry into the bonds its group takes from the parent's carbon for build.js and search.js alike.
- **The acid round trip reads the I-39c esters written `alcoxi` + `oxo`** (`ácido 4-metoxi-4-oxobutanoico`): the tiles have
  written them since R-5c1, and the engine names them so. A name with an ester prefix (`acetiloxi`, `metoxicarbonil`) is
  R-6a's (`ESTER_PREFIX`).
- **Deliberate, tested nulls** (diagnose.js «Acids», for I-51d3), each reason read independently in the tests: the
  student's suffix not an acid's beside the engine's –COOH (`1-hidroxipropanal`, `carboxiciclohexano`,
  `1-oxopropan-1-ol`: `seniority`); another number of –COOH groups (`ácido 3-carboxipropanoico`, `ácido
  2-carboxiciclohexano-1-carboxílico`, `ácido 4-(1,2-dicarboxietil)ciclohexano-1-carboxílico`: `count`); the –COOH's
  carbon on the other side of the parent (`ácido propano-1-carboxílico`, `ácido ciclohexilmetanoico`, `ácido
  fenilmetanoico`: `carbon`); the –COOH groups numbered otherwise (N0: `ácido ciclohexano-1,5-dicarboxílico`, `ácido
  benceno-1,5-dicarboxílico`: `numbering`); the suffix's needless locants (`ácido ciclohexano-1-carboxílico`, `ácido
  benceno-1-carboxílico`: `suffix`). Guarded too, though no case was found: P0 and `RINGCHAIN` (the parent chosen by the
  –COOH groups) and CHAIN_LOCANTS at N0. Their present messages would speak of OH or C=O groups.
