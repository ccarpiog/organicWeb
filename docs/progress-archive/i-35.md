## Decisions

- Acid part = parent chain with the C=O carbon as locant 1 (never cited), suffix `-oato`;
  its other groups are prefixes as for acids. O-bound group named with the substituent
  machinery, `-il` → `-ilo`; Spanish `<acid>oato de <alkyl>ilo`, English `<alkyl>yl <acid>oate`.
- Groups on the O-bound side (OH, C=O, halogens, ethers) are named inside the alkyl
  (`etanoato de 2-hidroxietilo`), as IUPAC does, rather than refused.
- `formiato de …` / `acetato de …` offered only for bare metanoato/etanoato, labelled like
  `ácido acético`; retained-name status from memory, not checked against the 2013 text.
- The explanation shows the English order with a fixed example ("methyl ethanoate"): the
  English lexicon is never bundled into the app.
- Existing tests that used an ester as the refused example now use an anhydride, a
  carbonate or acid + ester. A fixture check that assumed every multi-carbon molecule is
  numbered now allows a one-carbon acid part (methanoates).
- Diesters and ester prefixes (`alcoxicarbonil-`, `aciloxi-`) left to I-39; ring esters to I-40.
