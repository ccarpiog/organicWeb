## Risks to probe

1. **Verdict soundness:** `correct`/`alternative` must only be awarded when the assembled text
   (NFC) equals the engine's name or an alternative *for the built molecule* — never through the
   engine's rendering of the student's tree. Any path where a wrong multiplier, order, locant or
   di/bis could be silently repaired and awarded `correct` is a blocker.
2. **Build correctness:** chain and branch numbering (free-valence locant inside a branch,
   `iliden` = double bond to the parent, compound branches with their own prefixes, bis/tris,
   legacy locants mapped to the right segment), so that the built graph is the molecule the name
   describes. Check the off-by-one ranges: bonds 1…n−1, carbons 1…n, `BOND_RANGE` vs
   `LOCANT_RANGE`, valence counting with double/triple bonds and iliden.
3. **Tile ranges** of errors point at the offending tiles (the UI marks them).
4. **The `incomplete` stand-in** claims nothing; single-value left-out slots (`eteno`, `etenil`)
   filled directly are really single-valued.
5. **Injected namer** (used for the `outOfScope` and traditional-alternative tests because no real
   open-chain hydrocarbon refusal exists): the production path must not depend on it.
6. Purity: no DOM, bundler subset (`scripts/build.mjs` header: no cycles, no `import()`, no
   `import.meta`, no destructuring/multi-declarator exports, no live bindings); texts from the
   lexicon, not retyped. Spanish strings are drafts pending teacher review (not a finding).
7. Test strength: do the mutation tests assert both verdict and molecule (structural key)?
